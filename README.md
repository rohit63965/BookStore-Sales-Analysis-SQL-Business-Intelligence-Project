Project Overview -
A fictional bookstore wanted to understand its sales performance, customer purchasing behavior, inventory status, and revenue trends.
Using SQL, I analyzed transactional data to answer business questions that could help management improve pricing strategies, inventory planning, and customer retention.

Business Problem -
The bookstore collects data about books, customers, and orders, but lacks actionable insights.
This project demonstrates how SQL can transform raw transactional data into meaningful business intelligence.

Analytics - 
Analyze customer buying behavior
Monitor inventory health
Track monthly revenue
Identify top-performing books
Detect unsold inventory
Discover high-value customers
Compare pricing across genres
Perform advanced analytical reporting using SQL

Skills Demonstrated
SQL Query Basics – SELECT, WHERE, ORDER BY
Data Aggregation & Grouping – GROUP BY, HAVING, Aggregate Functions (COUNT(), SUM(), AVG(), MIN(), MAX())
Joins – INNER JOIN, LEFT JOIN
Advanced Queries – Subqueries, Common Table Expressions (CTEs)
Window Functions – Window Functions (OVER()), LAG(), LEAD()
Ranking Functions – ROW_NUMBER(), RANK()
Analytical Calculations – Running Totals
Business Analytics – KPI Analysis, Sales Analysis, Customer Analysis, Revenue Analysis, Trend Analysis

Business Insights
 1 - Revenue Analysis
Calculated total bookstore revenue and tracked cumulative monthly sales to evaluate business growth.

 2 - Customer Analysis
Identified high-value customers based on spending and purchasing frequency.

 3 - Inventory Analysis
Detected books with low stock and books that have never been sold, enabling smarter inventory planning.

 4 - Product Analysis
Analyzed bestselling books, genre performance, pricing distribution, and author-wise sales.

 5 - Time Series Analysis
Measured monthly revenue trends and month-over-month growth using window functions.
